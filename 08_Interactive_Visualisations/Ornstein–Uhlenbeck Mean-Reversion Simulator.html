What the simulator is teaching

All paths are generated client-side with Euler–Maruyama. Metrics update live when you move the sliders.

Stationarity

As t → ∞ the OU process converges in law to N(μ, σ²/(2θ)), independent of X₀. Variance stops growing — unlike a random walk whose variance grows linearly with time.

Mean-reversion speed θ

Larger θ pulls X back to μ more aggressively. The expected gap decays as E[X_t − μ] = (X₀ − μ) e−θt. Double θ and the process forgets its starting point twice as fast.

Half-life t½ = ln(2)/θ

Time for the expected deviation from μ to halve. At t = t½ the residual is 50 % of the original gap; at 2·t½ it is 25 %, and so on.

Long-run variance σ²/(2θ)

Higher σ widens the stationary cloud; higher θ shrinks it. The balance between noise injection and restoring force sets the equilibrium dispersion you see in the terminal histogram.

Process ≠ trading strategy

The SDE describes a statistical law of motion. A trading rule (e.g. “buy when below μ”) is an action on top of that law. Mean reversion of the process does not automatically imply positive expected P&L after costs, risk limits, or regime change.

vs Random walk / pure diffusion

A random walk dX = σ dW has no drift toward any level. Paths wander; variance grows without bound; there is no stationary distribution and no half-life. Overlaying both makes the restoring force visible.
