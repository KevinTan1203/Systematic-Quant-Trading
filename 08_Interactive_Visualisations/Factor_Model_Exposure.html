07Formulas
Linear Factor Model
R_i = α_i + β_i′ F + ε_i
F is a vector of factor returns. β_i are factor loadings. ε_i is residual (idiosyncratic) return, assumed uncorrelated across assets in this lab.
Portfolio Exposure
β_p = Σ_i w_i β_i    α_p = Σ_i w_i α_i
Factor Contribution to Expected Return
Contribution_k = β_{p,k} · λ_k
λ_k is the assumed factor premium. E[R_p] ≈ α_p + Σ_k β_{p,k} λ_k.
Risk Decomposition
Var(R_p) = β_p′ Cov(F) β_p + Σ_i w_i² σ²(ε_i)
R² ≈ Var_systematic / Var_total
08Concepts
Beta & alpha

Beta measures sensitivity of an asset (or portfolio) to a factor: a market beta of 1.2 means the asset tends to move 1.2% when the market factor moves 1%, other factors held fixed in the model. Alpha is the intercept — average return not explained by the chosen factors. Positive alpha in-sample is not proof of skill; it can reflect missing factors, noise, or overfitting.

Systematic vs idiosyncratic risk

Systematic risk is driven by factor shocks shared across assets and does not diversify away when holdings load on the same factors. Idiosyncratic (residual) risk is asset-specific; under the model’s independence assumption it shrinks as the portfolio diversifies. Real residuals can be correlated — another reason this lab is educational rather than a production risk system.

Factor premia & unintended bets

A factor premium is the expected return attached to bearing that factor’s risk. Even a “stock picking” portfolio can embed large unintended factor bets (e.g. accidental size or momentum exposure). The exposure bar chart makes those bets visible so they can be sized deliberately or hedged.
