05Formula Reference
Discount Factor — Annual Compounding
DF(T) = 1 / (1 + y(T))^T
y(T) is the annually compounded zero rate to maturity T (years).
Discount Factor — Semi-Annual Compounding
DF(T) = 1 / (1 + y(T)/2)^(2T)
Discount Factor — Continuous Compounding
DF(T) = e^(−y(T)·T)
Simply Compounded Forward Rate (T₁ → T₂)
DF(T₁) = DF(T₂) · (1 + f·τ)   with   τ = T₂ − T₁
f = ( DF(T₁)/DF(T₂) − 1 ) / τ
This f is the simply compounded rate for the period [T₁,T₂] implied by today’s discount curve. Convention is labelled in the UI.
Annually Compounded Forward (alternative form)
(1+y₂)^T₂ = (1+y₁)^T₁ · (1+f_ann)^(T₂−T₁)
Used when the annual compounding convention is selected for display consistency with input zeros.
Par Yield (annual coupon)
c = (1 − DF(T)) / Σ_{i=1…N} DF(t_i)
06Concepts
Time value of money & discounting

A dollar received in the future is worth less than a dollar today when positive interest rates prevail. The discount factor DF(T) is the present value of 1 unit paid at T. Multiplying any future cash flow by DF(T) converts it to today’s value under the chosen curve and compounding convention.

Spot / zero rates

The zero (spot) rate y(T) is the yield on a zero-coupon instrument maturing at T. It is in one-to-one correspondence with DF(T) once the compounding convention is fixed. Quoting zeros is often more intuitive than quoting discount factors; pricing engines typically work with DFs internally.

Forward rates are implied, not guaranteed

The forward rate from T₁ to T₂ is the break-even future rate that equates rolling a T₁ zero into a T₁→T₂ investment with holding the T₂ zero. It is locked in by today’s bond prices (under no-arbitrage assumptions). Realised future spot rates can — and usually do — differ. Forwards embed expectations, risk premia and convenience yields; they are not pure forecasts.

Why changing one zero moves forwards

Because f depends on the ratio DF(T₁)/DF(T₂), raising the longer zero (lowering DF(T₂)) increases the implied forward, while raising the shorter zero compresses it. Editing the table makes this dependence visible in the forward curve immediately.
