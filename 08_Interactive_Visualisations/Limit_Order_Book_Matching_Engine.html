05Microstructure Formulas
Best Bid / Offer & Mid
Best Bid = max{p : bid quantity at p > 0} · Best Ask = min{p : ask quantity at p > 0}
Mid = (Best Bid + Best Ask) / 2 · Spread = Best Ask − Best Bid
Spread (bps) = Spread / Mid × 10 000.
Order Book Imbalance
Imbalance = (Q_bid − Q_ask) / (Q_bid + Q_ask)
Using size at the best levels (or top-N). Near +1 = heavy bid pressure; near −1 = heavy ask pressure. Often used as a short-horizon directional signal.
VWAP & Slippage
VWAP = Σ (price_i × qty_i) / Σ qty_i
Slippage (buy) = Avg Fill Price − Mid_pre · Slippage (sell) = Mid_pre − Avg Fill Price
Positive slippage means you paid more (buy) or received less (sell) than the pre-trade mid — the cost of demanding liquidity.
Price-Time Priority
1. Better price always matches first.
2. At the same price, earlier timestamp (FIFO queue) matches first.
Your queue position = size ahead of your order at that price level. Partial fills reduce remaining quantity but preserve time priority for the residual.
06How the Book Works
Maker vs Taker

Maker orders add liquidity — they rest on the book and wait. Limit orders that do not cross the spread are makers. Makers typically receive a rebate on many exchanges because they tighten spreads and deepen the book.

Taker orders remove liquidity — they cross the spread and execute immediately against resting orders. Market orders and marketable limit orders are takers. Takers usually pay a fee.

In this simulator, fills against resting liquidity are marked as taker for the aggressor and maker for the resting side.

Limit vs Market Orders

A limit order specifies the worst price you will accept. A buy limit at 99.50 will never pay more than 99.50; a sell limit at 100.50 will never sell below 100.50. If the limit does not cross the book, the order queues at that price.

A market order has no price limit — it executes against whatever is available on the other side, walking the book level by level until filled or the book is exhausted. Market orders guarantee execution (if size exists) but not price.

Price-Time Priority & Queue Position

At each price, orders form a FIFO queue. The first order to arrive at that price is first to be filled when a counterparty hits the level. Your queue position is the aggregate size of orders ahead of you at your price. Improving the price (bidding higher / offering lower) jumps you to the front of a new level — that is how “queue jumping” works.

Partial Fills & Walking the Book

If you market-buy 50 and the best ask only has 30, you fill 30 at the best ask and continue to the next ask level for the residual 20 — this is called walking the book. Each level can produce a partial fill. Your average fill price is the size-weighted average across all levels touched. Large market orders relative to displayed depth create slippage and move the mid.

Spread, Depth & Impact

The spread is the immediate round-trip cost of aggressing (buy at ask, sell at bid). Depth is how much size sits at each level; thin depth means even modest market orders move the price. Cumulative depth charts show how far the price must travel to fill a given size — a direct visualisation of market impact.
