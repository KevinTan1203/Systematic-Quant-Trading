08Formula Reference
SDE
dSt = μ St dt + σ St dWt
Constant drift and volatility proportional to the level — the defining GBM structure.
Exact solution
St = S₀ exp[(μ − ½σ²)t + σ Wt]
Follows from Itô on ln S. Implies St is lognormal and strictly positive.
Exact simulation step
St+Δt = St exp[(μ − ½σ²)Δt + σ √Δt · Z]   ·   Z ∼ N(0,1)
Matches the true transition law of GBM — no Euler bias on the marginal distribution.
Theoretical moments
E[St] = S₀ eμt   ·   Var(St) = S₀² e2μt(eσ²t − 1)
Median is S₀ exp[(μ − ½σ²)t] — below the mean when σ > 0 (right skew).
Log-return law
ln(St/S₀) ∼ N((μ − ½σ²)t , σ² t)
This is why Black–Scholes can work with a normal kernel on log-prices.
09Educational Explanation
Brownian motion, drift, and volatility

Wiener process Wt is the continuous-time limit of random walks: continuous paths, independent Gaussian increments, quadratic variation growing like t.

Drift μ is the expected instantaneous rate of return. It shifts the log-mean by (μ − ½σ²) after the Itô correction.

Volatility σ scales diffusion intensity. Instantaneous variance of returns is σ² dt; uncertainty compounds with √t for log-returns.

Lognormal prices & continuous paths

Because S is an exponential of Brownian motion with drift, prices stay positive and the distribution is right-skewed: rare large upside outcomes pull the mean above the median. Sample paths are continuous almost surely — GBM cannot produce jumps or overnight gaps.

Limitations of GBM as a market model
  • Constant σ — real markets show volatility clustering and smiles/skews.
  • No jumps — crashes and discrete news moves are missing.
  • Lognormal tails — often thinner or differently shaped than empirical equity tails.
  • Single factor — no stochastic rates, dividends, or regime shifts.
  • Still the baseline for teaching, Black–Scholes, and many Monte Carlo engines.
