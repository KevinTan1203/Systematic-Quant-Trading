06Formulas
Order Book Imbalance (OBI)
OBI = (V_bid − V_ask) / (V_bid + V_ask)
V_bid and V_ask are aggregate displayed sizes over the top L levels. OBI ∈ [−1, +1]. +1 = only bids in the window; −1 = only asks.
Mid, Spread, Microprice
Mid = (BestBid + BestAsk) / 2 · Spread = BestAsk − BestBid
Microprice ≈ (BestAsk · Q_bid + BestBid · Q_ask) / (Q_bid + Q_ask)
Microprice weights the touch by opposite-side size and often sits closer to the side with less size (the side more likely to be hit next).
Short-Horizon Mid Return
r_{t→t+h} = Mid_{t+h} − Mid_t
Used in the response diagnostic: average subsequent mid change conditional on OBI exceeding a threshold at time t.
07How to Read Imbalance
OBI is descriptive, not a guaranteed predictor

Order-book imbalance summarises the current shape of displayed liquidity. In many markets and models, elevated OBI has historically shown a statistical association with short-horizon mid moves — because market orders that arrive next are more likely to consume the thinner side, or because informed traders lean the book before price adjusts.

That association is not a guarantee. It can flip sign under inventory-management regimes, spoofing, hidden liquidity, or when the apparent imbalance is itself the residual of fleeting cancel/replace activity. Treat OBI as a feature, not a signal to trade blindly.

How market orders consume depth

An aggressive buy market order matches the best ask, then walks further ask levels if size remains. That reduces V_ask, pushes OBI upward (more bid-heavy), and — if the best ask is depleted — shifts mid higher. The same logic applies symmetrically for sell aggression. This mechanical channel is one reason OBI and short-horizon returns co-move in the simulation.

Cancellations & fleeting liquidity

High cancellation rates make depth unreliable: size you see may vanish before you can take it. OBI computed on displayed size then becomes noisier, and “bid-heavy” readings can evaporate within ticks. The High Cancel scenario demonstrates how the OBI series becomes more volatile and less persistent.

Choosing the OBI depth window

L = 1 uses only the touch — reactive to queue dynamics and flicker. Larger L smooths over deeper levels and captures structural lean, but reacts more slowly to near-touch depletion. Explore the “Levels in OBI” slider to see how the same book produces different OBI paths under different windows.
