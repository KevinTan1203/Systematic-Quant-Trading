08Formula Reference
Portfolio expected return
μp = w′μ = Σi wᵢ μᵢ
Linear in weights — diversification does not change the weighted average of expected returns.
Portfolio variance
σp² = w′Σw = Σi Σj wᵢ wⱼ Cov(rᵢ, rⱼ)
Quadratic form. Off-diagonal covariances are the entire diversification mechanism.
Portfolio volatility
σp = √(w′Σw)
Not equal to Σ wᵢσᵢ in general. Equality holds only in the limit ρij → 1 for all pairs (or a single-asset portfolio).
Correlation ↔ covariance
Cov(rᵢ, rⱼ) = ρij σᵢ σⱼ   ·   ρij = Cov(rᵢ, rⱼ) / (σᵢ σⱼ)
Correlation is the scale-free version of covariance; both live in the covariance matrix Σ.
Component risk contribution
RCᵢ = wᵢ · (Σw)ᵢ / σp   ·   Σᵢ RCᵢ = σp
Euler allocation of portfolio risk. Percent of risk = RCᵢ / σp.
Diversification benefit
Benefit = (Σᵢ wᵢ σᵢ) − σp
How much volatility is “saved” relative to a perfect-correlation benchmark with the same weights.
09Educational Explanation
Covariance, correlation, and the matrix Σ

Covariance measures co-movement in the same units as variance (return²). Positive covariance means assets tend to move together; negative means they move opposite.

Correlation rescales covariance into [−1, +1]. ρ = +1 is perfect lockstep; ρ = −1 is perfect opposition; ρ = 0 is linear independence (not necessarily statistical independence).

The covariance matrix Σ stacks every pairwise Cov(rᵢ, rⱼ). It must be symmetric and positive semi-definite (all eigenvalues ≥ 0) for portfolio variance to be non-negative for every weight vector. This lab projects invalid matrices onto the nearest PSD matrix when needed.

Why weighted-average volatility is not portfolio volatility

Expected return is linear: μp = Σ wᵢμᵢ. Volatility is not. Expanding σp² = Σᵢ wᵢ²σᵢ² + Σi≠j wᵢwⱼρijσᵢσⱼ shows the cross terms. When ρij < 1 those terms shrink variance relative to the all-ρ=1 case, which collapses exactly to (Σ wᵢσᵢ)².

Hence σp ≤ Σ wᵢσᵢ, with equality only under perfect positive correlation (or degenerate portfolios). That gap is the diversification benefit.

What happens as ρ → −1, 0, +1
  • ρ → +1 — assets behave like scaled copies of one risk factor. Diversification disappears; σp approaches the weighted average of asset vols.
  • ρ → 0 — linear co-movement vanishes. Variance is dominated by the diagonal terms Σ wᵢ²σᵢ²; risk falls materially below the weighted average.
  • ρ → −1 — (especially with two assets) offsets can cancel. In the classic two-asset case with equal vol and equal weight, σp → 0. With more than two assets a single common ρ = −1 is often not PSD; the lab projects to the nearest usable matrix.
Process vs strategy — a caution

These identities describe statistical risk under a fixed mean vector and covariance. They are not a trading strategy. Realized correlations change in stress (often rising toward +1), expected returns are estimated with error, and leverage, costs, and constraints alter the outcome. The lab is a sandbox for the math of diversification, not a portfolio recommendation.
