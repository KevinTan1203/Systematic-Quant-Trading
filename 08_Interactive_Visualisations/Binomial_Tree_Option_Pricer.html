What The Tree Is Teaching
    Concepts & Formulas
    Risk-Neutral Probability
    u = e^(σ√Δt) d = 1/u p = [e^((r−q)Δt) − d] / (u − d)

    p is not the real-world probability of an up move — it's the probability under which the discounted expected stock price grows at the risk-free rate net of dividends. It exists purely so that pricing by expectation gives an arbitrage-free value; it is derived from u, d, r, and q, never assumed or observed from historical data.

    Replication & No-Arbitrage

    At each node, the option's payoff over the next step can be exactly replicated by holding Δ shares of stock and lending/borrowing cash at rate r. Because that replicating portfolio matches the option's payoff in both the up and down states, its cost today must equal the option's value today — otherwise a riskless arbitrage would exist. Risk-neutral pricing with probability p is a shortcut that produces the same answer as building this replicating portfolio at every node.

    Backward Induction
    V(i,j) = e^(−rΔt) · [ p·V(i+1,j+1) + (1−p)·V(i+1,j) ]

    Terminal payoffs are known exactly at expiry. Working backward one step at a time, each node's value is the discounted, probability-weighted average of its two children. By the time the recursion reaches the root, every intermediate node has already been priced — the root value is the option's price today.

    Early Exercise (American Options)
    V(i,j) = max( continuation value, exercise value )

    For American options, at every node the holder compares immediately exercising (intrinsic value) against continuing to hold (the discounted expected value shown above), and takes whichever is larger. Early exercise can be optimal for calls on stocks with large dividends, and for puts when interest rates are high enough that the present value of receiving the strike now outweighs the option's remaining time value.

    Convergence to Black-Scholes

    As the number of steps N grows, Δt = T/N shrinks, and the binomial distribution of terminal log-returns approaches a normal distribution by the Central Limit Theorem — the same lognormal assumption underlying Black-Scholes. In the limit N→∞, the CRR binomial price converges to the Black-Scholes-Merton closed-form price for European options. Convergence is not perfectly monotonic: odd and even step counts can approach from opposite sides, which is why the chart above often shows a slight zig-zag before settling down.
