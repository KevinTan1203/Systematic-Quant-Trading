    Formulas & Interpretation
    Delta (Δ) & Gamma (Γ)
    Δ_call = e^(-qT)·N(d1) Δ_put = e^(-qT)·(N(d1) - 1) Γ = e^(-qT)·n(d1) / (S·σ·√T) (same for call and put)

    Delta is the option's directional exposure: the expected $ change in the option for a $1 move in the underlying, and a rough proxy for the probability of finishing in-the-money. Gamma is the rate of change of Delta — it is largest when small moves in spot cause large relative changes in the likelihood of finishing ITM, which tends to be near-the-money with limited time to expiry.

    Vega
    Vega = S·e^(-qT)·n(d1)·√T (per 1.00 change in σ, shown per 1%)

    Vega measures sensitivity to implied volatility. Because it scales with √T and is maximized where n(d1) is largest (near d1≈0, i.e. near-the-money), longer-dated at-the-money options tend to carry the most volatility exposure in dollar terms — though this depends on r and q shifting the ATM point away from K=S.

    Theta (Θ)
    Θ_call = -[S·e^(-qT)·n(d1)·σ/(2√T)] - rK·e^(-rT)N(d2) + qS·e^(-qT)N(d1) Θ_put = -[S·e^(-qT)·n(d1)·σ/(2√T)] + rK·e^(-rT)N(-d2) - qS·e^(-qT)N(-d1)

    Theta is the change in value from one day passing, all else equal. For most long option positions Theta is negative and grows larger in magnitude as expiry nears, since less time remains for favorable moves. This is a general tendency, not a universal rule: theta can turn positive for some deep in-the-money European puts when r is high, since the discounted strike received grows faster than the remaining time value lost.

    Rho (ρ)
    Rho_call = K·T·e^(-rT)·N(d2) (per 1.00 change in r, shown per 1%) Rho_put = -K·T·e^(-rT)·N(-d2)

    Rho measures sensitivity to the risk-free rate. It is generally larger in magnitude for longer-dated, deeper in-the-money options, since the present value of the strike (paid for calls, received for puts) is more sensitive to discounting the further out and further ITM the option is.
