01 / The five layers

Who, where and how a trade becomes an asset

Follow the economic and operational stack from the investor initiating an order to legal ownership and ongoing mark-to-market.

i
01 / Participants

Retail investors, asset managers, hedge funds, corporates, banks and market makers create demand, supply and risk transfer.

02 / Intermediaries

Brokers act as agents; dealers trade as principals; prime brokers provide financing, securities lending and consolidated reporting.

03 / Trading venues

Exchanges, electronic venues, ATSs and bilateral RFQ networks discover and match orders under different transparency rules.

04 / Clearing

CCPs can novate a cleared trade, collect initial and variation margin, and mutualise extreme losses through a default fund.

05 / Settlement & custody

CSDs record legal ownership while custodians safekeep assets, process corporate actions and support ongoing valuation.

05 / Counterparty-risk management

Why central clearing and margin exist

Novation reduces bilateral network complexity, but it concentrates operational and default-management responsibility in the CCP ecosystem.

Margin stack
Initial marginVariation marginDefault fund
Initial margin

Upfront collateral calibrated to potential close-out loss over the margin period of risk.

Variation margin

Daily or intraday cash flow covering mark-to-market changes so exposure stays near zero.

Default fund

Mutualised resource that absorbs losses beyond a defaulting member's own margin.

A CCP changes the counterparty relationship; it does not make market, liquidity or operational risk disappear.

07 / Professional application

Why market structure matters to a quant workflow

The venue and post-trade choices determine what data exists, how an order can be executed and where residual risk sits.

Research
Data

Venue selection changes timestamps, depth, survivorship, corporate-action treatment and the completeness of the sample.

Development
APIs

Trading systems must integrate market data, order routing, execution reports, clearing status, settlement and custody records.

Trading
Access

The product, counterparty, margin model and settlement timeline constrain which strategies can actually be deployed.

Common beginner mistake: assuming every market is as transparent and liquid as listed equities. OTC price discovery, settlement and counterparty risk require a different mental model.
