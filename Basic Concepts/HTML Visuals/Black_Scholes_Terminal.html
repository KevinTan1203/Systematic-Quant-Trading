Formulas & Explanations
Black-Scholes-Merton Pricing Formula

The model prices a European option as the discounted, probability-weighted payoff under a risk-neutral measure.

C = S·e^(-qT)·N(d1) - K·e^(-rT)·N(d2) P = K·e^(-rT)·N(-d2) - S·e^(-qT)·N(-d1) d1 = [ln(S/K) + (r - q + 0.5σ²)T] / (σ√T) d2 = d1 - σ√T

N(·) is the standard normal cumulative distribution function. Intuitively, N(d2) is the risk-neutral probability the option finishes in-the-money, while the S·e^(-qT)·N(d1) term is the present value of the asset received conditional on exercise.

Intrinsic Value vs. Time Value

Intrinsic value is what the option would be worth if exercised immediately: max(S−K, 0) for a call, max(K−S, 0) for a put. It can never be negative.

Time value is the remainder of the price: Price − Intrinsic Value. It reflects the possibility that the option becomes more valuable before expiry, and it decays to zero as T → 0 (theta decay). Time value is maximized for at-the-money options and shrinks deep in- or out-of-the-money.

Delta & Gamma
Δ_call = e^(-qT)·N(d1) Δ_put = e^(-qT)·(N(d1) - 1) Γ = e^(-qT)·n(d1) / (S·σ·√T) (same for call and put)

Delta is the option's sensitivity to a $1 move in the underlying, and approximates the hedge ratio (shares to hold per option) and the option's directional probability of finishing ITM. Gamma is the rate of change of delta — it measures how quickly the hedge ratio shifts as the spot price moves, and is largest for at-the-money, near-expiry options.

Vega, Theta & Rho
Vega = S·e^(-qT)·n(d1)·√T (per 1.00 change in σ) Θ_call = -[S·e^(-qT)·n(d1)·σ/(2√T)] - rK·e^(-rT)N(d2) + qS·e^(-qT)N(d1) Θ_put = -[S·e^(-qT)·n(d1)·σ/(2√T)] + rK·e^(-rT)N(-d2) - qS·e^(-qT)N(-d1) Rho_call = K·T·e^(-rT)·N(d2) Rho_put = -K·T·e^(-rT)·N(-d2)

Vega measures sensitivity to implied volatility. Theta measures the daily erosion of time value as expiry approaches (usually negative for long option holders). Rho measures sensitivity to the risk-free rate.

How Each Input Moves the Price
InputIncrease → CallIncrease → PutWhy
Spot S↑ higher↓ lowerA higher spot raises the payoff of a call and lowers the payoff of a put at expiry.
Strike K↓ lower↑ higherA higher strike makes calls harder to finish ITM and puts easier.
Volatility σ↑ higher↑ higherMore uncertainty raises the value of optionality in both directions (limited downside, unlimited upside).
Time to Expiry T↑ higher↑ higher (usually)More time means more chance for a favorable move; time value is generally larger, though deep ITM puts with high rates can be an exception.
Risk-free Rate r↑ higher↓ lowerA higher rate lowers the present value of the strike paid (helps calls) and lowers the PV of the strike received (hurts puts).
Dividend Yield q↓ lower↑ higherDividends reduce the expected forward price of the underlying, hurting calls and helping puts.
