08Formula Reference
Latent Markov chain
P(St=j | St−1=i) = Pij   ·   St ∈ {1,…,K}
The regime is hidden. Only returns are observed. Diagonal Pii large ⇒ persistent regimes.
Gaussian emissions
Rt | St=j ∼ N(μj, σj²)
Annual μ,σ are converted to per-step mean μ/spy and vol σ/√spy.
Forward filter (predict–update)
αt|t−1 = P⊤ αt−1|t−1   ·   αt|t ∝ diag(ℓt) αt|t−1
ℓt(j) = φ(Rt; μj, σj) is the emission density. Normalize αt|t to sum to 1.
Expected regime duration
E[duration | state i] = 1 / (1 − Pii)
Geometric holding time under constant exit probability 1−Pii.
Stationary distribution π∞
π∞ = π∞ P   ·   π∞ 1 = 1
Left eigenvector of P for eigenvalue 1 (when the chain is irreducible).
09Educational Explanation
Hidden states vs observed returns

The regime St is a latent discrete variable. You never observe it directly — only Rt (or prices). Different regimes change the conditional distribution of returns (mean and volatility). Clustering of high-volatility episodes is a hallmark of regime-switching vs a single Gaussian.

Transition probabilities & persistence

Pij is the probability of moving from i to j in one step. Large diagonal entries mean regimes are sticky: once in “Crisis,” you tend to stay there for a geometrically distributed number of days. That produces volatility clustering without needing GARCH.

Why HMMs help with regime detection
  • They formalize “market state” as a probabilistic object with dynamics.
  • Filtering/smoothing yields time-varying regime probabilities useful for risk, allocation, or signal design.
  • They can generate realistic multi-modal return distributions and sudden risk-on/risk-off shifts.

Limitations: discrete regimes are a simplification; parameters must be estimated (EM/Baum–Welch) in practice; misspecified K or Gaussian emissions can mislead.

Filtering vs the true path

This lab knows the true parameters, so the filter is the optimal online belief. In real applications you estimate P, μ, σ first; estimation error blurs regime calls. Even with known parameters, filter uncertainty remains when regimes have overlapping emission densities (similar μ,σ).
