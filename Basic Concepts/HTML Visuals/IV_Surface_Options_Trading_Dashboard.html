08Formula Reference
Synthetic IV surface
m = ln(K/S)   ·   ATM(T) = ATM₀ + slope · √T
IV(K,T) = max(σfloor, ATM(T) + skew · m + curvature · m²)
Simple pedagogical parameterization. Real surfaces are arbitrage-constrained and calibrated to quotes.
Black–Scholes–Merton
d₁ = [ln(S/K)+(r−q+½σ²)T] / (σ√T)   ·   d₂ = d₁ − σ√T
C = S e−qT Φ(d₁) − K e−rT Φ(d₂)   ·   P = K e−rT Φ(−d₂) − S e−qT Φ(−d₁)
Greeks (calls; puts via put–call)
Δc = e−qT Φ(d₁)   ·   Γ = e−qT φ(d₁) / (S σ √T)
Vega = S e−qT φ(d₁) √T   ·   Θ, ρ standard BSM forms
Vega reported per 1% IV move (÷100). Theta per day (÷365).
25Δ risk reversal & butterfly
25Δ RR = IV25Δ call − IV25Δ put
25Δ BF = ½ (IV25Δ call + IV25Δ put) − IVATM
RR measures skew; BF measures smile curvature / wing premium.
Sticky-strike vs sticky-delta
Sticky-strike: IV'(K) = IV(K) after spot move
Sticky-delta: IV'(K) = IV(K · S/S')   (smile moves with moneyness)
09Educational Notes
What the IV surface represents

Implied volatility is the σ plugged into Black–Scholes that matches a given option price. Plotting IV across strikes and expiries yields a surface. Skew and term structure encode the market’s distributional and path assumptions beyond constant-vol GBM.

Why equity skew is typically negative

Crash fears and demand for downside protection lift OTM put IVs relative to OTM calls. In the parametric model, a negative skew coefficient on m = ln(K/S) raises IV for K < S.

Sticky-strike vs sticky-delta

When spot jumps, the surface must be re-marked. Sticky-strike keeps IV fixed by absolute strike (short-run trader rule of thumb). Sticky-delta keeps IV fixed by delta/moneyness so the smile slides with spot. Real behavior is a blend and varies by horizon and asset class.

Limits of this synthetic model
  • No calendar/vertical arbitrage constraints enforced.
  • No discrete quoted grid, bid–ask, or interest-rate curve.
  • Single local BS price per (K,T) — not a full local-vol or SV model.
  • For learning surface shape, Greeks, and sticky rules only.
