08Concepts
Smile vs skew

A smile is the pattern of IV against strike (or moneyness) at fixed expiry — often U-shaped in FX and some equities. Skew refers to the asymmetric slope: in equity index markets, low strikes (puts) typically carry higher IV than high strikes (calls), producing a downward-sloping smile in strike space. Skew is often quoted via risk reversals.

Term structure

ATM IV across expiries forms the term structure. Upward-sloping term structures are common when the market prices more uncertainty at longer horizons or mean-reverting short-dated vol; inverted term structures often appear when near-term event risk dominates. This lab uses a simple log-linear tilt of ATM vol — illustrative only.

Risk reversal & butterfly

RR25 isolates the skew component between 25Δ call and put vols. BF25 isolates the average wing richness versus ATM (curvature / smile convexity). Traders watch both as compact summaries of the surface slice.

High IV is not automatically expensive

Implied vol is the market’s pricing kernel for optionality — it embeds expected future realised vol, risk premia, supply/demand for hedges, and distributional assumptions (tail risk). An elevated IV may be “fair” or even cheap if realised volatility and jumps are likely to be higher still. Valuation requires a view on realised dynamics and relative value across strikes/expiries, not IV level alone.
