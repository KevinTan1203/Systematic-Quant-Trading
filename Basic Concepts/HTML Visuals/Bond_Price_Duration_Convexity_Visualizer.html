06Formula Reference
Bond Price (periodic)
P = Σk=1…N C / (1 + y/f)k + Face / (1 + y/f)N
C = (coupon rate × Face) / f · N = maturity × f · y = annual YTM · f = payments per year. Assumes settlement on a coupon date.
Macaulay Duration
MacD = (1/P) · Σk tk · PV(CFk)
Weighted-average time to receipt of cash flows, weights = PV of each cash flow / price. Measured in years.
Modified Duration & DV01
Dmod = MacD / (1 + y/f)
DV01 ≈ Dmod · P · 0.0001
Modified duration scales Macaulay duration so that −Dmod × Δy approximates the percentage price change. DV01 is the (approximate) dollar change for a 1 basis-point rise in yield.
Convexity (periodic)
Conv = (1/P) · Σk [ tk(tk+1/f) · PV(CFk) ] / (1 + y/f)²
Second derivative of price w.r.t. yield, scaled for use in the percentage-change formula below.
Taylor Approximations
ΔP/P ≈ − Dmod · Δy
ΔP/P ≈ − Dmod · Δy + ½ · Convexity · (Δy)²
Δy is the decimal yield change (e.g. 0.01 for +100 bp). Duration is the first-order (linear) term; convexity supplies the second-order correction that captures the curvature of the price–yield curve.
07Why Duration & Convexity Matter
Duration is a first-order approximation

Modified duration tells you the slope of the price–yield curve at the current yield. For an infinitesimal yield change the linear (tangent) approximation is exact. For any finite move the curve’s convexity means the true price change differs from −Dmod·Δy. Because the price–yield relationship is convex (curves upward), duration systematically underestimates the price rise when yields fall and overestimates the price decline when yields rise.

Convexity improves the estimate

Adding the ½·Convexity·(Δy)² term captures the curvature. For moderate shocks (roughly ±50–150 bp for typical coupon bonds) the two-term expansion is already very accurate. Residual error comes from third- and higher-order derivatives of the price function. Longer-maturity, lower-coupon bonds have higher convexity and therefore benefit more from the second-order correction.

Coupon, maturity, duration & convexity relationships

Higher coupon → more cash flow arrives earlier → lower Macaulay duration and lower convexity.

Longer maturity → cash flows stretched further out → higher duration and higher convexity (all else equal).

Higher yield → heavier discounting of distant cash flows → lower duration and convexity.

Zero-coupon bonds have Macaulay duration equal to maturity — the maximum possible for a given final payment date.

DV01 in practice

DV01 (dollar value of one basis point) is the risk-management workhorse: it converts duration into a cash sensitivity that can be summed across positions. A portfolio DV01 of $50 000 means an approximate $50 000 loss for a parallel +1 bp yield rise (and the opposite for a fall). Because it is still a linear approximation, large yield moves or pronounced curve reshaping require convexity or full revaluation.
