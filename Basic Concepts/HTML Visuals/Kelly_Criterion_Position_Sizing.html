07Formulas
Kelly Fraction (binary bet)
f* = (b·p − q) / b    with    q = 1 − p
b = net fractional odds (profit per unit staked on a win). If b = 1 (even money), f* = 2p − 1.
Expected Log Growth
g(f) = p · ln(1 + f·b) + q · ln(1 − f)
Kelly maximises g(f). Wealth after n rounds scales asymptotically as e^{n·g} in the typical (median) sense under i.i.d. bets.
Wealth Update
Win: W ← W · (1 + f·b)    Loss: W ← W · (1 − f)
Requires f < 1 so that a loss does not instantly zero the bankroll (unless f = 1 and b finite).
Fractional Kelly
f_λ = λ · f*    (λ = 0.25, 0.5, …)
Reduces growth rate roughly proportional to λ near the peak, but reduces variance and drawdowns faster than linear — the practical appeal of half-Kelly and similar rules of thumb.
08Concepts
What Kelly optimises

Kelly maximises the expected logarithm of wealth — equivalently, the asymptotic exponential growth rate of a bankroll facing repeated bets with a known edge. It does not maximise expected wealth (which for a favourable bet is pushed toward betting everything). Log utility heavily penalises paths that approach zero.

Why full Kelly can hurt

Even with a true edge, full Kelly paths are extremely volatile. Large drawdowns are common; the median outcome can lag the mean because the distribution of terminal wealth is right-skewed. If the edge is overestimated, full Kelly becomes overbetting: g(f) drops and the probability of severe losses rises sharply. Fractional Kelly is a pragmatic response to uncertainty and finite-horizon path risk.

Overbetting

Any f > f* is overbetting: you accept more volatility and lower long-run growth. In the growth chart, the curve declines past the peak. 1.5× Kelly is included so you can see this penalty directly in the Monte Carlo results.

Estimation error

Use the “p noise” control to size bets with an optimistic p̂ while the true win rate is lower. This is one of the main reasons practitioners avoid full Kelly: the optimum is knife-edged under misspecification. When noise is high, smaller fractions dominate on median wealth and drawdown metrics.

Mathematics vs recommendation

The simulator’s f* is exact for the stated binary model with known p and b. Translating that into a portfolio weight for equities, opaque strategies, or correlated bets requires additional assumptions (continuous returns, covariance, constraints). Treat Kelly here as a teaching device about growth, variance and overbetting — not as a prescription for live capital allocation.
