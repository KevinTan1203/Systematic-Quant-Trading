10Formula Reference
Merton Jump Diffusion SDE
dSₜ / Sₜ = (μ − λk) dt + σ dWₜ + (J − 1) dNₜ
The instantaneous return decomposes into compensated drift, continuous diffusion, and a compound Poisson jump term.
Discrete-Time Simulation Step
S(t+Δt) = S(t) · exp[(μ − λk − ½σ²)Δt + σ√Δt · Z] · ∏ Jᵢ, i = 1..N(Δt)
Z ~ N(0,1) drives diffusion; N(Δt) ~ Poisson(λΔt) draws the jump count; each Jᵢ is an independent jump multiplier.
Jump Multiplier
ln(J) ~ N(μ_J, σ_J²)
Jump sizes are log-normal: the log of the multiplier is normally distributed with mean μ_J and standard deviation σ_J.
Expected Jump Multiplier
E[J] = exp(μ_J + ½σ_J²)
Follows from the log-normal moment-generating function.
Jump Compensation
k = E[J] − 1
k is subtracted from the drift (as λk) so that the jump component has zero expected instantaneous contribution — this is the compensated-drift form used in the simulation, distinct from the raw physical-measure drift μ alone.
Poisson Jump-Count Process
N(t) ~ Poisson(λt)
The number of jump events in an interval of length t is Poisson-distributed with mean λt.
11Educational Explanation
How the Jump Diffusion Model Works
Component 1 — Drift

Drift μ represents the expected directional movement of the asset per year, absent any randomness. In the jump-adjusted simulation, the effective drift used inside the exponential is compensated by −λk so that jumps don't silently add extra expected return on top of μ.

Component 2 — Diffusion

The continuous part, σ dWₜ, models everyday random fluctuations via a Wiener process (Brownian motion). It produces the familiar smooth, "jittery" GBM-style path with no discontinuities.

Component 3 — Jump Process

Real markets occasionally move by a discrete, sudden amount rather than drifting there gradually. Component 3 layers a compound Poisson process on top of the diffusion to capture events such as:

  • Earnings surprises
  • Economic announcements
  • Geopolitical shocks
  • Market crashes
  • Unexpected news

Standard GBM assumes a continuous sample path, so no matter how volatile σ is, it cannot reproduce an instantaneous discrete price gap. Jump diffusion adds exactly that missing ingredient by letting the Poisson counter N(t) "fire" and multiply the price by an independent, log-normally distributed jump size J.

Physical-Measure Drift vs. Compensated Drift

This tool simulates in the physical (real-world) measure, using your specified annual drift μ directly. The compensated drift term (μ − λk) is a modeling device, not a change of probability measure: it subtracts the expected contribution of jumps, λk, from μ so that the diffusion coefficient inside the exponential doesn't need to separately account for jump-driven expected return.

In a genuine risk-neutral formulation (used for derivatives pricing), the drift would instead be set so that the discounted asset price is a martingale, i.e. μ would be replaced by (r − λk) where r is the risk-free rate, and jump risk may additionally carry a risk premium depending on market completeness assumptions. This console does not perform derivatives pricing and does not assume market completeness — it uses the physical-measure formulation with drift compensation applied only to the jump term, as shown in the SDE above.
